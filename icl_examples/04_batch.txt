1. Introduction

The electrocardiogram (ECG) is the recording of the cardiac activity obtained by a noninvasive technique, a record of the bio-electric potentials corresponds with the contractions and relaxations of the heart muscle. ECG is useful for the diagnosis, discovery and cure of cardiac diseases. However, for the sampled ECG signal, it is predictable to mix various noises such as power-line interference, baseline wander, muscle noise and motion artifact and other noises with, which in varying degrees causes misjudgment and omission of conventional ECG identification for the ECG features extraction and reduces the diagnostic accuracy. Moreover, with the recent telemedical applications involving transmission and storage of ECG, noise also appears due to poor channel conditions. A noisy ECG may hinder the physician's correct evaluations on patients. Therefore, the ECG signal denoising and preprocessing become an exclusive requirement.

In the literature, many approaches have been reported to address ECG enhancement. Among them, adaptive filter architecture is common [1]. Statistical techniques such as principal component analysis [2], independent component analysis [3,4], and neural networks [5] were used to extract a denoised signal from the noisy ECG. A supplementary factor is that, presently signal-processing strategies are typically based on band pass filters (BPFs), and are implemented using separate hardware and microprocessors. Consequently, with these techniques it is hard to control the signal processing performance because there are too many signal-processing units involved [6].

Nie et al. [7] and Behrenbruch and Lithgow [6] independently experimented with new signal processing strategies based on wavelet transform (WT) in order to simplify and minimize the hardware, because WT inherently supports all the features of signal processing provided by separate hardware and microprocessors but in fewer units. A multi-resolution characteristics based denoising technique using wavelet transform (WT) have also acknowledged among the researchers in recent years. In WT the decomposed wavelet coefficient is thresholded and after that reconstructed using Inverse Discrete Wavelet Transform (IDWT).

Most of the studies focused their attention to remove very common sources of noises like white noise and baseline wander. Baseline wandering removal has been addressed in the literature individually. Baseline estimation using cubic spline [8], baseline construction by linearly interpolating between pre-known isoelectric levels estimated from PR intervals [9], linear filtering [10], and the use of wavelet packets [11] and wavelet based Wiener filtering techniques [12] are the major approaches in this field.

In Refs. [13,14] denoising were only done in the case of white noise. However, ECG signals are also contaminated with other wide range of noises which is a major concern. Another problem of choosing threshold value arises. Many methods exist for choosing the value of threshold and S-median [14] is one of them. Although [14] gives a novel S-median algorithm for choosing the threshold, it has some drawbacks. They used the tuning factor b as unity in most of the cases, but optimal value of b is desirable for denoising. Another factor is that level dependent adaptation factor gives better result which is absent in the existing S-median thresholding algorithm.

In this research, we also proposed a novel algorithm named modified S-median to find an adaptive value of the threshold and compared with other existing techniques. The basic aim of the proposed thresholding is to improve the quality of the ECG signal. This algorithm combines both the important issues: the noise-free reconstruction property and level-dependent adaptation factor which makes it more sophisticated for removing the noise. Symlet wavelet function has been selected for coefficient calculation for modified S-median thresholding. According to [15], Symlet wavelet was chosen to remove the noise from ECG signal as its scaling function and wavelet function is similar to ECG waveforms. Furthermore, the analysis was not only done in the time domain using different statistical tools like percent root mean square difference (PRD), mean square error (MSE), normalized MSE (NMSE), root mean square error (RMSE) and SNR improvement but also done in frequency domain and time-frequency domain.

The outlines of this study are as follows. Section 2 provides brief characteristics of different noises and their implementation. In this section, theoretical background on the different wavelet transform and modified S-median threshold are also presented. Section 3 is dedicated to the quantitative and qualitative evaluation of the techniques. Finally, discussion and conclusion are provided in Section 4.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Data acquisition

2.1.1. Experimental recording of ECG acquisition

An experiment was performed to collect the data for this research work. The subject was male of 26 years old with no known cardiovascular disorder. For ECG measurement, required equipments are BIOPAC data acquisition unit (MP36), BIOPAC electrode lead set (SS2L), BIOPAC disposable vinyl electrodes (EL503) with cable and power [16]. During experiment white lead was placed on right forearm, red lead on the left leg and the black lead was placed on right leg as shown in Fig. 1. Subject was seated in a chair relaxing and asked to be as still as possible to ensure lower motion artifact and EMG signal on the data. After running calibration sequence, ECG data was recorded. The total experiment was performed in the biomedical signal processing laboratory, Khulna University of Engineering and Technology.

2.1.2. ECG acquisition from MIT-BIH arrhythmia database

MIT-BIH arrhythmia database [16] was also used to obtain real ECG signal. It consists of 48 annotated records, obtained from 47 subjects studied by the arrhythmia Laboratory of Beth Israel Hospital in Boston between 1975 and 1979. About 60% of the records were obtained from inpatients. The database contains 23 records (the '100 series') chosen at random from a set of over 4000 24-h Holter tapes. Several records in the 100 series were chosen for our research purpose. Each record in the arrhythmia database is slightly over 30 min in length having sampling frequency of 360 Hz. 'Header' files include information about the leads used, the patient's age, sex, and medications [17].

2.2. Different noises under study

When an electrocardiogram is recorded, it would be contaminated with many kinds of noise and interferences. However, the major sources of noise like white noise, colored noise, real muscle artifact, electrode movement and composite noise were studied in this research.

2.2.1. Synthetic noise

White noise is a random signal with a flat power spectral density. It has all frequency components. Color noise or flicker noise is a low-frequency electronic noise which is inversely proportional of power spectral density with respect to the frequency. Resistance fluctuation in the ECG recorder is the main reason for flicker noise generation. That is why all resistors have flicker noise.

In this study color noise is modeled by changing the slope of a spectral density function reduce with frequency, where f is the frequency, sigma^2 is the variance of the original signal, and beta is the slope that measures of noise color. White noise (beta = 0), pink or flicker noise (beta = 1), and brown noise (beta = 2), are three most frequently referenced noises. White noise and color noise are simulated having 5 dB input SNR using MATLAB.

2.2.2. Real noise

2.2.2.1. Electrode contact noise. Due to the loss of contact between the electrode and skin a transient interference occurred which is known by electrode contact noise. The loss of contact can be permanent, or can be intermittent, as would be the case when a loose electrode is brought in and out of contact with the skin as a result of movements and vibration. This switching action at the measurement system input can result large artifacts since the ECG signal is usually capacitively coupled with the system. With the amplifier input disconnected, 50 Hz interference may be significant. An exponentially decayed random and rapid baseline transition with a superimposed 50 Hz component is used to model electrode contact noise. This alteration may occur once or in a series. Characteristics of this noise signal include the amplitude of the initial transition, the amplitude of the 50 Hz component, and the time constant of the decay.

2.2.2.2. Motion artifacts. Motion artifacts are transient (but not step) baseline changes caused by changes in the electrode-skin impedance with electrode motion. As this impedance changes, the ECG amplifier sees a different source impedance, which forms a voltage divider with the amplifier input impedance. Therefore, the amplifier input voltage depends on the source impedance, which changes as the electrode position changes. The usual cause of motion artifacts will be assumed to be vibrations or movement of the subject. The shape of the baseline disturbance caused by motion artifacts can be assumed to be a biphasic signal resembling one cycle of a sine wave. The peak amplitude and duration of the artifact are variables.

2.2.2.3. Baseline drift. The frequency of respiration acted as sinusoidal component is added to the ECG and is called baseline drift. The amplitude varies with respiration is about 15%. The sinusoidal component is amplitude modulated with the ECG signal to generate this type of noise [18].

Real noises are extracted from the noise stress test database in MIT-BIH [16]. Low-amplitude muscle noise is common in ECG. Different noises were tested on the ECG data recorded from BIOPAC data acquisition system [15]. The noisy signal was generated by adding real baseline wander (RBW), real electrode movement (REM), and real motion artifact (RMA) (noise stress test database of MIT-BIH) and white and color noise were added to the ECG signals. Different types of noisy ECG signal are shown in Fig. 2.

2.3. Wavelet transform

2.3.1. Continuous wavelet transform

The wavelet is a scaled and shifted form of the main pattern, so called the "mother wavelet". In a nutshell, the continuous wavelet (CWT) is nothing but a set of the inner product of the observed signal f(t) with the shifted and scaled mother wavelets.

2.3.2. Discrete wavelet transform

As CWT is much redundant representation, it can be described using a dyadic or octave wavelet transform [19]. Now if we relate the signal expression point of view with filter bank point of view using multi-resolution analysis and filter bank theory, h(n) and g(n) are the low pass and high pass filter of the filter bank respectively. Therefore, DWT uses a set of dyadic scales and it is translated from the wavelet function to form an orthonormal basis for the signal analysis [21].

2.3.2.1. Wavelet filter. Using DWT, signal is decomposed by repeated filtering with a pair of filters; low pass filter (LPF) and high pass filter (HPF), and its cut-off frequency is the middle of input signal frequency [22]. A 2-level decomposition of orthogonal wavelet basis is illustrated in Fig. 3, that is, detail coefficients at all the two levels (D1 and D2) and approximate at deepest decomposition level (A2). Approximate coefficients often resemble the signal itself.

Now for calculating the wavelet coefficients, it needs to know the coefficient of low pass h(n) and high pass g(n) and these coefficients are coming from Symlet wavelet transform which is compactly supported with least asymmetry and the highest number of vanishing moments for a given support width [20]. Symlet is chosen in this study because of it similar to ECG waveforms [15].

2.3.2.2. Justification of choosing Symlet wavelet. Daubechies wavelet families are very asymmetric in nature because they are generated by choosing the minimum phase square root [23]. It can be shown that filters equivalent to a minimum phase square root concentrated their optimal energy near the initial point of their support [24]. Therefore Daubechies wavelets are non-symmetric. Daubechies proposes modifications of her wavelets that increase their symmetry can be increased while retaining great simplicity. To achieve a symmetric or anti-symmetric wavelet the conjugate mirror filter h(n) must be symmetric or anti-symmetric corresponding to the center of its support. It means that h(omega) has a linear complex phase [23]. Daubechies proved that the "Haar filter is the only real compactly supported conjugate mirror filter that has a linear phase" [25]. Therefore to obtain more symmetric filter of Daubechies (hence the name "Symlet") the choice of the square root should be optimized to get nearly linear phase [30]. The resulting wavelets should maintain minimum support with p vanishing moments like Daubechies wavelets but they are more symmetric. This modified version of Daubechies wavelet is known as Symlet wavelet and has significant contribution in signal and image processing and can preserve better spectral information [26]. Moreover, the shape of scaling function and wavelet function of Symlet wavelet are like the shape of ECG signal that makes better correlation and hence better denoising. Therefore, Symlet is used in this study. The scaling function and wavelet function are of Symlet are shown in Fig. 4.
